Seven days on the coast path

By a fictional walker · 14 min read

The path starts at the harbour wall and does not leave the sea for 140 kilometres. Each day is a climb out of one cove and a descent into the next, with a village, a café and a bus stop at roughly the right intervals.

We walked it in late September, after the school holidays and before the ferries cut back to the winter timetable. The weather held for six days out of seven.

Day one: harbour to headland

A gentle start along the old tramway, then the first real climb up to the lookout. The view back over the bay is the one on every postcard in the harbour shops, and it earns its place.

The descent is steep and loose in places. Poles help. The campsite at the bottom has hot showers and sells bread in the morning.

Day four: the long one

Twenty-four kilometres and the most climbing of the week. Start early, carry more water than you think, and do not count on the kiosk at the lighthouse being open.

The last two kilometres are flat, along the cliff top, with the lighthouse in sight the whole way. It never seems to get closer.

What we would change

Lighter boots, one fewer jumper, and a rest day at Fisher's Bay. The tide tables matter more than the forecast on days five and six, where the path drops to the beach.

Point End has a bus back to the harbour twice a day. The 16:40 is the one to aim for.
